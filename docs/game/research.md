# Research notes: the political strategy genre

Method: text sources only (Steam store pages, storefront and database listings, written reviews, gameplay guides, forum threads). No videos.

## Lawgivers II (SomniumSoft / Damian Bernardi, Early Access Aug 2023)
- Turn-based political and geopolitical **sandbox**. You start as an activist or as a party leader.
- **Parliament:** you pass and abolish laws, elect a speaker, and set up committees. You can bypass parliament, but that has consequences.
- **Electoral systems:** proportional, majoritarian or mixed, and lawmakers can propose to change the system. Constituencies are editable, so gerrymandering is possible. First-past-the-post with auto-generated constituencies arrived in a patch.
- **Lawmakers** hold their own opinions. A party with too many differing views can split or lose members.
- **Three win paths:** personal interest, party influence, national prestige.
- **Goal:** win elections and avoid national bankruptcy (budget pressure).
- Uses a world map with real countries and subdivisions. The developer plans a 1950 start; I could not verify any "eras" content in text sources.
- Reception: "Mostly Positive" on Steam (~500 reviews at snapshot). Complaints I could find (some about the first Lawgivers) are about poor onboarding and "empty clicking".
- Sources: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1407180/Lawgivers_II/ , https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/12311205 , https://somniumsoft.itch.io/lawgivers-ii , https://rawg.io/games/lawgivers-ii , https://dekudeals.com/items/lawgivers-ii

## Democracy 4 (Positech)
- Policies sit in a web of sliders. Each policy shifts the happiness of about 20 overlapping voter groups and also moves simulation values such as crime and air quality.
- **Overlapping membership:** a voter belongs to several groups, so a group being happy does not guarantee its votes.
- **Cynicism** comes from election-eve pandering and from flip-flops. **Complacency** also matters.
- Criticism: the game can feel too easy or swingy ("win with >90 or lose with <10"), and it reads as cold and numeric.
- Sources: https://gamepretty.com/democracy-4-basic-gameplay-guide/ , https://gameplay.tips/guides/8590-democracy-4.html , https://forums.positech.co.uk/t/some-initial-thoughts-on-game/16202 , https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/democracy-4 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_(video_game)

## Suzerain (Torpor Games)
- A narrative presidency built on binary and multiple-choice decisions. Choices interlock, and nearly every choice carries a monetary, political or personal cost.
- Advisors preview the likely outcomes. A single autosave makes choices permanent.
- Criticism: some players find the choice range illusory, closer to a visual novel than a simulation.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game) , https://opencritic.com/game/10629/suzerain/reviews , https://www.noobfeed.com/reviews/suzerain-pc-review , https://chalgyr.com/2020/12/review-pc-suzerain.html

## What makes the genre fun (synthesis)
1. **Legible trade-offs.** Every law pleases someone and angers someone. The fun is in reading the web of effects (Democracy) and committing to a cost (Suzerain).
2. **The vote count as drama.** Whipping a narrow coalition and watching the tally land on 36 or 37 is the genre's best moment, and it comes from Lawgivers' focus on parliament.
3. **Elections as a scoreboard with a geography.** Regions make support concrete and give you targeting decisions (campaign where?).
4. **Scarcity.** Treasury and political capital stop you doing everything. Bankruptcy is a real fail state (Lawgivers).
5. **Events break the plan.** Crises force reactive choices with no clean answer.
6. **Memory and consequence.** Cynicism and flip-flop penalties make the past matter.
7. **Pain points to avoid:** poor onboarding and empty clicking (Lawgivers), opaque or swingy maths (Democracy), illusory choice (Suzerain), and run lengths of hours. Answers in this design: tooltips with a forecast of each effect, small numbers, a 24-turn cap, and a forecast vote tally before every roll call.
